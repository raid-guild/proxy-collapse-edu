## Page 1 — One Sheep, One Prompt

### Story purpose

Establish the seductive simplicity of personal AI: give it words, receive work. Reveal that the human still carries continuity, context, judgment, and responsibility.

### Suggested beats

1. A steward writes a precise instruction and hands it to a lone sheep.
2. The sheep completes the task impressively.
3. The steward repeats the same instruction; the result changes unexpectedly.
4. The steward adds more words and qualifications.
5. Instruction cards pile up while the sheep waits.
6. The steward realizes they are still personally holding the entire system together.

### Candidate animated panel

The same instruction is run three times, producing three subtly incompatible outcomes. The final still is the sheep surrounded by conflicting result cards.

### Page turn

The steward says: “Maybe it needs to know what I know.”

## Bridge 1 — Prompt Engineering Is Not a System

### Core idea

Prompt engineering controls an instruction, not the surrounding organization. The bridge distinguishes an instruction from durable intent, policy, state, and acceptance criteria.

### Deeper points

- Why larger prompts move human overhead rather than eliminating it.
- The hidden work of remembering history and checking results.
- Prompt quality matters, but cannot replace tools, permissions, evidence, or continuity.
- Metric introduction: human review minutes per accepted outcome.

### Bridge visual

A simple diagram showing the prompt inside a much larger boundary labeled context, state, tools, policy, and evidence.

## Page 2 — The Backpack

### Story purpose

Introduce context engineering as deliberate selection rather than indiscriminate accumulation.

### Suggested beats

1. The steward gives the sheep a backpack containing a map, policies, history, and task records.
2. With focused context, the sheep takes the correct path.
3. The steward adds every available document “just in case.”
4. The backpack grows enormous and the sheep slows down.
5. Contradictory maps spill onto the road.
6. The steward removes irrelevant material and labels separate pockets by purpose.

### Candidate animated panel

A context slider fills the backpack: too little leaves the sheep lost, enough produces a clear path, and too much buries the sheep beneath paper.

### Page turn

The sheep now knows where to go, but reaches a locked tool shed with no safe way to act.

## Bridge 2 — Context Is a Selection Problem

### Core idea

Context engineering decides what a role can know during a run. More context is not automatically better.

### Deeper points

- Relevance, freshness, authority, and provenance.
- Focused context versus missing context versus overload.
- Split context as both a coordination technique and a security boundary.
- Durable organizational memory should not be confused with putting everything into one model window.

### Bridge interaction

An optional three-position context control: starved, focused, overloaded. A static comparison must provide the same lesson.

## Page 3 — The Harness

### Story purpose

Move from a clever responder to a bounded organizational worker. The sheep receives tools, permissions, completion rules, state, and evidence capture.

### Suggested beats

1. The steward fits the sheep with a simple harness.
2. The harness contains a key ring, tool slots, a task ledger, and a receipt pouch.
3. The sheep tries the wrong key; the harness blocks access and records the attempt.
4. It uses the allowed tool and marks the task complete.
5. A judge asks for proof; the sheep presents a receipt instead of a confident claim.
6. The steward inspects the evidence and adjusts one boundary.

### Candidate animated panel

An unsafe tool action reaches a red permission boundary, stops, and produces an exception receipt. The sheep then selects the permitted reversible action.

### Page turn

The steward observes that one safe run is useful—but repeatable work needs a path.

## Bridge 3 — Tools Need Boundaries and Receipts

### Core idea

A harness combines tools, permissions, state, completion rules, failure behavior, and evidence for one run.

### Deeper points

- Capability is not permission.
- Completion claims versus completion evidence.
- Reversibility, scoped credentials, budgets, and escalation behavior.
- Why auditability only reduces review when evidence maps to acceptance criteria.

### Bridge visual

An exploded illustration of the sheep’s harness, with each component labeled and tied to the human burden it removes.

## Page 4 — The Loop

### Story purpose

Show work becoming repeatable and governance beginning to contract from constant approval toward exceptions.

### Suggested beats

1. The sheep enters a marked route: Work → Judge → Verify → Improve.
2. At first, a human approval gate blocks every station.
3. A queue forms and otherwise routine work stops.
4. The steward writes explicit acceptance criteria and installs deterministic checks where possible.
5. Ordinary evidence passes through; an ambiguous case turns red and leaves the loop.
6. The steward handles that exception while the bounded routine continues.

### Candidate animated panel

The loop repeatedly stalls at approval gates. Evidence checks appear one by one, routine work begins to flow, and one clearly marked exception still routes to the human.

### Page turn

The loop works—but one sheep cannot carry every specialized role without mixing contexts and permissions.

## Bridge 4 — From Human in Every Step to Governance by Exception

### Core idea

Governance minification reduces routine review without reducing accountability. Review gates are engineering hypotheses, not permanent rituals.

### Deeper points

- Routine review versus review justified by a concrete unresolved failure mode.
- Every gate should have a reason, trigger, and retirement condition.
- Trust is earned within a stable risk class through measured performance.
- Useful measures: review minutes, exception rate, escaped defects, and cost per accepted outcome.
- Consequential changes remain visible, attributable, and reviewable.

### Bridge visual

A governance-surface diagram comparing three stages: blanket approval, evidence-backed review, and exception handling.

## Page 5 — The Governed Flock

### Story purpose

Introduce multiplayer agent systems and separated roles without portraying the flock as an unbounded autonomous swarm.

### Suggested beats

1. A complex request arrives at a shared boundary.
2. An orchestrator sheep divides it into durable tasks.
3. Specialist sheep receive different backpacks and tools.
4. Each produces an artifact and evidence for the next role.
5. A judge sheep evaluates the result; a verifier independently checks the receipt.
6. The steward can inspect who knew what, changed what, and passed which evidence.

### Candidate animated panel

A request travels through the flock. As it reaches each sheep, only that role’s context, permissions, and evidence channels illuminate.

### Page turn

The day’s work ends. The flock sleeps, but the evidence remains awake.

## Bridge 5 — Multiplayer Agents Need Shared Intent and Split Context

### Core idea

Shared agent systems require organizational infrastructure: durable requests, workflows, artifacts, permissions, execution history, and common intent.

### Deeper points

- Why multiple large prompts do not create an organization.
- Separated orchestration, execution, judging, and verification contexts.
- Coordination cost and when specialization is worth it.
- The organization owns memory and policy; individual runs consume scoped portions.
- Use a sanitized SuperPrism workflow as the implementation example.

### Bridge visual

A role-and-evidence routing map with an inspectable boundary around the entire flock.

## Page 6 — The Dream Cycle

### Story purpose

Resolve the title metaphor. “Dreaming” is asynchronous consolidation of traces, evidence, friction, and possible improvements—not uncontrolled self-rewriting.

### Suggested beats

1. The flock sleeps beneath a dark field of disconnected evidence.
2. Related traces begin to form a cyan constellation.
3. Repeated friction appears as a bright recurring pattern.
4. The system drafts a proposed change to a reusable harness specification.
5. The proposal remains visibly separate from canonical policy and history.
6. At dawn, the steward reviews the provenance and releases a verified improvement to the next flock.

### Candidate animated panel

Night falls; memory nodes connect with provenance lines. A proposed harness change descends to a review table at dawn while the historical evidence remains untouched.

### Final image

The sheep begins another loop with a lighter, better-fitted harness. The boundary is still visible, the evidence stations remain, and the steward is still present.

### Closing line

**The goal was never a sheep without a shepherd. It was a system that knew when the shepherd was needed.**

## Closing note — Improving the Source Without Rewriting the Past

The final note connects the dream cycle to the SuperPrism source/target pattern:

1. A target organization performs real work.
2. Execution evidence reveals recurring friction.
3. A change is proposed against the reusable source template or harness specification.
4. The change is tested and verified separately.
5. A reviewed release becomes available to target instances.
6. Local policy, memory, and history remain locally governed.
